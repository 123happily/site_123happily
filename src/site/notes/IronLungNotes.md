I saw Iron Lung twice!
The second time, with subtitles in a different theatre. And to keep from being so terribly scared, I took notes. The *whole time*. I promise I wasn't being a nuisance to other people in the cinema.

Because of that, I think I put together a few things that wouldn't be obvious from a first watch, especially if you're like me and have difficulty hearing dialogue in movies.

This assumes you've already seen Iron Lung; both the game and the movie, and particularly the lore update to the game.

**Simon**
My best note-taking on one of his flashbacks:
His hands covered in blood with child's (?) bodies collapsed around him. He looked pretty young in that scene. A mentor giving him a radio, or a detonator. A redhead yelling at him to do something before an explosion floods down the corridor and envelops him. Voices telling him to ‘find it’.

That redhead shows up later as a hallucinated corpse in the sub, potentially both times was the same guy. At least, I'm pretty sure that's who it is. I'm also debating whether this is the same 'brother' who left the message in the sub, since the ghost/vision led Simon to it.

I think Simon's role as the 'butcher' was working for Eden to provide bodies to be used as soil to grow / maintain their trees. At the very least he killed a lot of people perhaps at a young age, we see him surrounded by bodies looking at the blood on his hands. But he doesn't agree with the plan to destroy Filament Station, yet is unable to prevent it.
"When did you grow a conscience, Simon? You’ve got the highest bodycount out here!"

I think Simon made a deal with the devil for eternal life in the midpoint of the film, that's the symbolism I'm seeing anyway. The hallucinatory voice from the SM-8 asks him what he'd give to survive, and he says 'everything. I never had anything in the first place' looking at the glass charm. Later, the creature simply tells him "agreed".

That glass charm, I think it's got a petrified leaf or maybe even a germinating tree seed inside of it. He ties it onto his wrist, then in the dream-sequence-scene confronting the blood god his arm is tied down by that same string. It sinks into the blood, it's tethering him... I don't know what it means but it's cool!

Something about his eyes is important. The voices during the final act just says 'your eyes', and we see them flash red once at least, maybe twice. Including at the end right before the explosion/flash of light that rips apart the sub and the creature both.

God I wish I remembered his wording, whether it was 'I'll go to prison' or 'I'll go *back* to prison'. Because the official timeline of events puts Filament Station's destruction 5 years after the quiet rapture, and Simon is notably quite young during that time; so has he been aging for like 20 years until the movie starts???? This is based on numbers from the lore update of the game so I wouldn't be surprised or even really mad if they ignored those for the story.

**Eden + The Father**
From the game's lore update, it says Eden is a station with 468 people and the 'only known remaining trees' ("We wont be returning to the grove, brother")

Eden seems like a religious place - name aside. 'do you believe in god?' 'I hear enough of that talk on Eden'. It also seems to be a brothership - the Father, likely the leader of Eden, refers to the group we see Simon in as his 'sons'. They in turn refer to each other as 'brothers'. I personally think they're not all biological brothers, on ground of there being too many of them (lol).

The Father is the source of the opening monologue. ‘The consolidation hides its technology, its people’. He mentions that the COI knows that one of the blood moons is different to the rest, and I think it's implied that the COI is trying to hide that knowledge.

The kids were clearly indoctrinated from a very young age though, told about the stars being gone and fed Eden's mentality (whatever that is). I think they're the canon recipients of the monologue, including the lines about their bodies becoming the soil (for the tree(s))

I can't get a clear read on Eden's motive. ‘No-one on Eden wants to think this way, but \[what if it was the stations and ships that went missing, not the planets, and everyone else is just wondering where we went]’ I wonder why Eden doesnt want to think that... I guess their belief is that humanity is OVER over. Or at least that hope is dangerous/bad. Or maybe just that they shouldn't try to mess with the origin of the Quiet Rapture. With the religious stuff, maybe they think of it as god's plan...?

'It's all over, that's what Eden understands' also doesn't tell me much more. I don't understand their motivation for attacking Filament, or siccing Simon as the Butcher on people.

**COI, broadly**
I think the COI's knowledge about the blood moon is more limited than expected. They're surprised when Simon finds a skeleton, even more so when he says something is alive down there. Then again, they knew this moon in particular was potentially important, so that surprise might have been faked.

I think if they could be remotely piloting these missions, they absolutely would.

The game lore says they're quite defensive and scared of Eden, meanwhile Eden is bitching that the COI doesn't share more information and resources, so the conflict is pretty understandable.

**Themes, broadly**
There's a lot of dichotomies in this movie. Determination to survive, realising things are more important than your life, finding absolution/serving penance for your actions, staying true to your promises. Simon is holding desperately to his will to live right up until the end of the movie. *That's* what breaks in him.
‘Sorry brother, but I wanna live’
'I'm not dying down here'
‘Would you give everything just to survive?’ ‘Yeah. I never had anything to begin with’
He's offered eternal life by the spectres of the dead speaking for a god beyond comprehension. I don't think that was a hallucination.
Then, eventually, Ava coming down after him and being killed, then the god putting the responsibility for that on Simon breaks him. He chooses faith in Ava who lied to him over and over, he chooses self sacrifice; 'It's more than me'
