I saw Iron Lung twice!
In two different theatres, and I got a subtitle machine the second time. And to keep from being so terribly scared, I took notes. The *whole time*. I promise I wasn't being a nuisance to other people in the cinema.

What follows is my ramblings about things I found interesting without getting into anything outside of cold, hard lore. That said, I haven't 'figured out' all the answers. I think that's an intentional part of the cosmic horror. If the unknowableness frustrates you, I don't think you'd like Iron Lung anyway lol

This assumes you've already seen Iron Lung; both the game and the movie, and optionally the lore update to the game. This story works on mysteries and suspense, so don't let me spoil it for you. I won't be explaining any of that anyway!

### Simon
I think Simon's role as the butcher was working for Eden to provide bodies to be used as soil to grow or maintain their trees. "They think there’s no soil (but) when we die… our bodies become the soil" "How many bodies have you climbed over to keep yourself alive". At the very least, even at a young age, we see him surrounded by bodies with blood on his hands. He disagrees with Eden about the plan to attack Filament Station, yet is unable, or perhaps unwilling, to prevent it, which ends in its destruction.

That glass charm, I think it's got a petrified leaf or a germinating tree seed inside of it. I think his mum gave it to him, but it could have come from one of his 'brothers' or Eden generally. He ties it onto his wrist, then in the dream-sequence-scene confronting the Demon, his arm is tied down by that same string. It sinks into the blood, and he can't pull it free... I don't know what it means, but it's cool! It might be something about holding onto hope in the face of despair, or holding onto foolish beliefs in the face of truth. Being stuck halfway between two choices.

Something about Simon's eyes is important. The voices during the final act just say "your eyes", and we see them flash red at least once, maybe twice. Including at the end right before the flash of light that rips apart the sub and the Demon both. I swear there's similar red flashing in the 'sky' in his hallucination sequence. Maybe it's to do with the Light though that's kind of golden yellow. Something about Simon is definitely 'special' though.

To really reach for more details, I think he might have been an environmental technitian of a sort. He rebukes Ava's explanation of the water being condensation, quote "I know what condensation (looks like)". Refusing to read the manual. ... and I swear someone on the radio mentioned that humanity was running out of skills and knowledge on the topic of balancing risk sending people, including Simon, down. "They wouldn't have put CO2 scrubbers in this thing" (?) is my last flimsy point to add. It could be cute characterisation or I could be losing my mind already.
### Eden
From the game's lore update, it says Eden is a station with 468 people and the 'only known remaining trees' likely referenced in the line "We wont be returning to the grove, brother"

Eden seems like a religious place - name aside.
SM-8 crew: "Do you believe in God?"
Simon: "I hear enough of that talk on Eden"
The Father, probably the leader of Eden, refers to the group we see Simon in as his "sons". They in turn refer to each other as "brothers". I personally think they're not all biological brothers, on grounds of there being too many of them (lol).

The Father is the source of the opening monologue. "The Consolidation hides its technology, its people". He mentions that the COI knows that one of the blood moons is different to the rest, and I think it's implied that the COI is trying to be sneaky about it.

"No-one on Eden wants to think this way, but \[what if it was the stations and ships that went missing, not the planets, and everyone else is just wondering where we went]" "It's all over, that's what Eden understands" My best guess is that Eden sees the Quiet Rapture as part of God's plan and probably don't believe in reverting it. I wonder how much Simon agrees with this.

David says he read Simon's file, knows that he doesn't buy all of Eden's "bullshit", "all that dog(talk?) about the last tree". Eden can't be straight doomerism since they're clearly working to preserve *some* life. Perhaps their own lives over others, with some religious justifications.

### Filament Station
One of the COI's stations, maybe their biggest, that Eden attacked. It's worth noting that Eden has a larger station themselves. Per the game's lore update, it was "a 9 day battle \[...] The conflict ended unresolved when Eden troops resorted to sabotaging the station's reactor, resulting in a critical breach". It doesn't seem like destruction was the plan.

We see a little of the event in Simon's flashbacks. A hooded figure giving him a radio, or a detonator. A redhead yelling at Simon to "Do something" before an explosion floods down the corridor and envelops him.

Simon talks a whole lot about it.

Ava: "62 others didnt make it out and that doesnt include your compatriots"
Simon: "I didn't want them to die"
"I didnt blow up Filament Station but the ones who did aren't being punished" "\[I] didn't have a choice" "I had nothing to do with Filament Station, that wasn't part of the plan, they didnt listen to me" "People I thought were my brothers abandoned me" "(I just) drew the short straw (trading one metal box to die in with another)" "Why does anyone care" "Filament Station is gone just like everything else in the Quiet Rapture"

We've got all the hallmarks of a guilty conscience. Shifting the blame away, victimising himself, diminishing the damage.

Redhead in his memory, I think: "When did you grow a conscience, Simon? You’ve got the highest bodycount out here!" "\[You'll have to] kill your own brothers to stop this"

So I think Simon got stuck between two bad choices and couldn't commit either way. I would even wager he made it worse because of that.
### Timeline Woes
I wish I remembered Simon's wording, whether it was "I'll go to prison" or "I'll go *back* to prison". The official timeline of events puts Filament Station's destruction 5 years after the Quiet Rapture, and Simon seemed quite young at that time; so do like 20 years pass before the movie starts? Time he'd ostensibly have spent in jail? Have the SM-8 crew, who don't know about Filament Station, really been down there for that long? It's entirely possible.

"You dream of going back to a life you can't have/that doesn't exist" plus Simon remembering the voice of his mother makes me curious whether Simon was born before the Rapture. Speaking of which,
### The Mother (officially "A Familiar Voice")
His mother seems like a cool character. I caught a line from who I believe is her about the COI or some other group confiscating tree seeds to "start a garden of their own", but that she'd snuck this one away (I'm less sure about this now). "We have a tree, it'll feel just like home. Don't be like that, you’ll love it there" which means different things depending on when Simon was born. Before the Rapture, and the 'home' is Earth, or after, and the 'home' is Eden. Regardless, I'm not sure why Simon was unenthusiastic about it. Maybe he just didn't believe her and couldn't share in her hope.

The SM-8 voices say "\[you dream of] going back to a mother who wouldn't recognise the killer she spawned. Or maybe she'd be proud". That's the main reason I think this is his mother at all, but more than this, she died *before* he turned into a monster. Which in itself seemed to happen at quite a young age. Maybe her death led him down that path.
### The COI (Consolidation)
I think the COI's knowledge about the blood moon is more limited than you'd expect. They're surprised when Simon finds a skeleton, even more so when he says something is alive down there. Then again, they knew this moon in particular was potentially important. So, what *did* they know about? The Light, I guess?

The game lore says they're quite defensive and scared of Eden, meanwhile Eden is bitching that the COI doesn't share more information and resources, hence the conflict.

David (Ava's second in command) says that Ava cares for everyone, albeit "some more than others". Whenever Simon accuses her of wanting him dead, she gets genuinely angry. I think if they could be remotely piloting these missions, they absolutely would. Despite what the Father says about them being secretive and exclusionary, Ava makes statements that imply Eden's people are included in her efforts to save humanity. And she does eventually apologise to Simon for being unable to save him. I can't really see her as evil.

### The Redhead (officially "The Guy Who Told You to Cross the Wires")
The guy who left the note, recording, and glass charm in the sub before Simon was put in. I think this is the redhead who shows up in Simon's flashback yelling at him to "do something". I think he's also the hallucinated corpse in the sub, since he leads Simon to his own note. He gets up from the chair the second time we see him - if this was a time mirroring situation, he should have been standing, like Simon was the first time he saw the ghost. This role isn't just a 'voice' role, so he has to be on screen at some point. I'm terrible at recognising faces, so that is my best evidence :)

At one point Simon asked Ava if anyone ever made it back up, and she simply said 'Yes' and didn't elaborate. At another point Ava mentions they've rescued subs before. I think this fellow chose to run out of oxygen ("I'll breathe my last") outside of the COI's influence ("uncontrolled") and they eventually recovered the sub, his body included. It's of course possible that they've had people return alive, perhaps with their minds broken, and/or recovered subs where people *didn't* "make it" back as well.

Though these are two people, I think they both waver between being themselves and being a mouthpiece for the Demon. You can almost plot their words between these two extremes.

### The Light
A fragment of what caused the Quiet Rapture. There was actually really interesting dialogue about it being the pinhole through which God, or *A* god views the universe, but I didn't manage to write it down. It shining through the hull I always interpreted as Simon driving his sub basically on top of it, though he doesn't strike me as the type to do that... but it does have an alternate-dimension feel that nothing else really does.

After the data from the Light is downloaded to Simon's black box, the box itself starts unendingly gushing blood - I'm certain of this, there's a huge long shot on it to show us exactly that. So perhaps the 'answers' that the light provides aren't just revelations, they can be boiled down to hard data, and then that data supernaturally has an effect just by existing. Pretty cool. "It burst our mind like a tick" is the wording the SM-8 crew used referring to the answers from the Light, and ticks fill up with blood. Very clever word choice.

It would indeed be a great source of infinite air, water, and food (given that it's oxygenated blood, most likely). Nevermind that "the blood is human, it's US!"

I suppose that raises the question: does the Light still produce blood now that there's at least one, maybe two, copies of the data producing blood? Not an important question per se so we shan't dwell on it (pro dwelling on it state championship runner-up tshirt)

In addition to this, "We only saw \[signs of human blood] with the samples we took from near the light". I guess that's handy to explain why they don't just use the blood already in the blood ocean. Honestly, the raging infections should have been clue number one. So, yay, only a little bit of the blood ocean is human! Hooray.

### The Demon (and the blood)
Whether it's one god of many, or *the* god, or an alien, I'm curious why we see both it and a skeleton that looks like the same species. That implies some multiples or at least a reincarnation-based lifecycle. (Note: It's an x-ray camera, so that might not have been a skeleton, but that raises confusion about why the actual Demon isn't a skeleton too. It's confusing either way)

The Demon doesn't want the truth about the light to get out. It wants Simon to destroy the data. "Just the light must be forgotten", I wonder why...
It's probably something to do with it being a window out to God - sure, maybe it's a resource battle or a power struggle, but how boring is that.

The Demon seems quite cunning as well. Considering “it wants you to do this”; it seems to be pushing/leading Simon to the SM-8 ruin even from the start. It disappears back down the tunnel he eventually has to go down, at least I think that’s what happens, like it’s asking Simon to follow it.

When Simon goes back for the SM-8 to download the data, it's moved, and shows up *I believe* behind him. Around this point the voices say "It's a trap". I’m still unsure what it wanted Simon to do, maybe join with it? Certainly to destroy the light data.

### Themes, broadly
The will to survive at any cost, balancing the values of lives including your own, finding absolution or serving penance, staying true to your promises, justice, freedom. There's a lot going on.

A quick aside before I get into my main point: A few times 'free' is implied to equal 'dead'. The Brother choosing to die "uncontrolled". The Demon voices offer that "You will live and we will be free" and later *I think* they say "give us our peace". To which Simon says "Fine, you want the butcher!?" if that's his reply to their plea for mercy killing it's a WILD way to do it, but maybe him killing them is freeing them. That line wasn't in the CC though so it's anyone's guess. Ava also promises him that he'll be "free" and though I do believe she intended to rescue him it certainly doesn't end that way. Just generally the comparisons of the blood ocean to Hell give the idea that death would be better. Conpared to this, the Demon's offer of eternal life also comes with implied imprisonment.

Another quick aside, there's no shortage of slightly Christian imagery throughout. I think Simon made a deal with the devil, or at least there's symbolism of the sort. The voice from the SM-8 asks him what he'd give to survive and he says "Everything". Later, the Demon simply says "Agreed". You could look at the bathing in blood as a kind of fucked up baptism. The Light as an angel, a God's viewpoint on the world, the Rapture with most of humanity gone into said Light to be 'served within us', Eden is... Eden. Other people have pointed out Simon making that cross pose and being a sacrificial saviour figure.

Simon clings to his will to live right up until the end of the movie:

"Sorry brother, but I wanna live"
"I'm not dying down here"
"Would you give everything just to survive?" "Yeah. I never had anything to begin with"
(multiple lines of bravado like "you're gonna have to come in here to kill me")

He has a strong sense for justice but doesn't apply it to himself much;

You see this in the microcosm of him irradiating the crew and David dropping the sub; "kinda fair after what you did". Simon accepts this wordlessly, and later apologises. He believes that reaction was fair. Yet, Simon yells at Ava that it's not 'fair' how he's being treated, despite having murdered plenty of people ("\[you've got] more blood on your hands than anyone out here, killer!" or whatever) even before Filament Station. His sense of justice is at odds with his desperation to live, because I mean, he's a *mass murderer*. Some countries straight up kill you for that.

I can't pick if "I think I've done enough" is an honest feeling, or him just begging to be let out. Maybe he truly felt redeemed at that stage, but I kind of doubt it.

The Demon offers answers and, more importantly, eternal life. "You will live" plus the 'living proof' in the SM-8 crew.
It insults him and plays with his insecurities, "Half measures… never enough". (By the way, I really want that soundbyte, it went so hard). His indecision likely caused the Filament Station disaster and seems to be a sore point.
Ava comes down for him and the Demon kills her, blaming Simon. "You took her down to hell with you" ("and now she'll die" or something). The Demon dangles absolution in front of him. "You can still redeem yourself, destroy the data, none can be allowed to see the light again." The Demon tries to break him down and then offer an 'out', threatening him with death, then offering salvation. Threatening him with guilt, then offering forgiveness.

Despite all of this, Simon chooses self sacrifice, to believe "It's more than me". I wonder if he felt truly redeemed.
### in conclusion

In the end, Simon's eye flashes red, the ship explodes with red light and some dark tendrils shoot outwards… I'd buy those being roots, though they had red light going along them. The Demon fell to pieces, which is interesting. And the box is found at the end. Simon might have genuinely saved humanity, provided the 'answers' don't drive everyone insane.

Just by the by, there's another angle on the Light/Demon/SM-8 crew love triangle, where the SM-8 crew actually live within the Light, and the Demon is just some worm thing. That's pretty compelling considering lines like "The light thinks and therefore we are" "We are so much, we can serve everyone within us, we are one, we live within". It could be a reason why the SM-8 crew talk about the Demon as a seperate entity. I'm torn between the two ideas so you'll have to forgive me for sticking my own spokes this late in the game.

I can't wait to pick through this movie on streaming.
