### Simon
I think Simon's role as the butcher was working for Eden to provide bodies to be used as soil to grow or maintain their trees. "They think there’s no soil (but) when we die… our bodies become the soil" "How many bodies have you climbed over to keep yourself alive". At the very least, even at a young age, we see him surrounded by bodies with blood on his hands. He disagrees with Eden about the plan to attack Filament Station, yet is unable, or perhaps unwilling, to prevent it, which ends in its destruction.

I see a lot of people talk about Simon's visions and the voices he hears as hallucinations of a mind broken by concussion, alcohol, dehydration, radiation poisoning, what have you. Though he absolutely does *go through it*, I actually think most, if not all, of these phenomena are real visions or real memories.

That glass charm, I think it's got a petrified leaf or a germinating tree seed inside of it. I think his mum gave it to him, but it could have come from one of his 'brothers' or Eden generally. He ties it onto his wrist, then in the dream-sequence-scene confronting the Demon, his arm is tied down by that same string. It sinks into the blood, and he can't pull it free... everyone online says there's roots going down into the blood, and I must be a brick cause I just did not see those. It might symbolise a connection to the blood ocean, or his past, that he can't escape from. It might be about hope or desperation stopping him from being truly 'free'. I'm not sure what it means, but it's cool!

Something about Simon's eyes is important. The voices during the final act just say "your eyes", and we see them flash red at least once, maybe twice. Including at the end right before the flash of light that rips apart the sub and the Demon both. I swear there's similar red flashing in the 'sky' in his vision.

This is me really reaching now, but, I think he might have been an environmental technitian of a sort. He rebukes Ava's explanation of the water being condensation, quote "I know what condensation (looks like)". Refusing to read the manual. ... and I swear someone on the radio mentioned that humanity was running out of skills and knowledge on the topic of balancing risk sending people, including Simon, down. "They wouldn't have put CO2 scrubbers in this thing" (?) is my last flimsy point to add. It could be cute characterisation or I could be losing my mind already.
### Eden
From the game's lore update, it says Eden is a station with 468 people and the 'only known remaining trees'.

Eden seems like a religious place - name aside.
SM-8 crew: "Do you believe in God?"
Simon: "I hear enough of that talk on Eden"
The Father, probably the leader of Eden, refers to the group we see Simon in as his "sons". They in turn refer to each other as "brothers". I personally think they're not all biological brothers, on grounds of there being too many of them (lol).

"No-one on Eden wants to think this way, but \[what if it was the stations and ships that went missing, not the planets, and everyone else is just wondering where we went]" "It's all over, that's what Eden understands" My best guess is that Eden sees the Quiet Rapture as part of God's plan and probably don't believe in reverting it. I wonder how much Simon agrees with this.

David says he read Simon's file, knows that he doesn't buy all of Eden's "bullshit", "all that dog(talk?) about the last tree". Eden can't be straight doomerism since they're clearly working to preserve *some* life. Perhaps their own lives over others, with some religious justifications.

### Filament Station
One of the COI's stations, maybe their biggest, that Eden attacked. It's worth noting that Eden has a larger station themselves. Per the game's lore update, it was "a 9 day battle \[...] The conflict ended unresolved when Eden troops resorted to sabotaging the station's reactor, resulting in a critical breach". It doesn't seem like destruction was the plan. I'd guess that they were after information that the COI was hiding.

We see a little of the event in Simon's flashbacks. A hooded figure giving him a radio, or a detonator. A redhead yelling at Simon to "Do something" before an explosion floods down the corridor and envelops him.

Simon talks a whole lot about it, and it sounds like a guilty conscience. Shifting the blame away:
Ava: "62 others didnt make it out and that doesnt include your compatriots"
Simon: "I didn't want them to die"
"I didnt blow up Filament Station but the ones who did aren't being punished" "\[I] didn't have a choice" "I had nothing to do with Filament Station, that wasn't part of the plan, they didnt listen to me"

victimising himself: "People I thought were my brothers abandoned me" "(I just) drew the short straw (trading one metal box to die in with another)"

 diminishing the damage: "Why does anyone care" "Filament Station is gone just like everything else in the Quiet Rapture"

Redhead in his memory, I think: "When did you grow a conscience, Simon? You’ve got the highest bodycount out here!" "\[You'll have to] kill your own brothers to stop this"

So I think Simon got stuck between killing his brothers and letting them do whatever it was on Filament Station because he couldn't commit either way. That indecision led to the destruction of the station.

### The Mother (officially "A Familiar Voice")
His mother seems like a cool character. I caught a line from who I believe is her about the COI or some other group confiscating tree seeds to "start a garden of their own", but that she'd snuck this one away (I'm less sure about this now). "We have a tree, it'll feel just like home. Don't be like that, you’ll love it there" which means different things depending on when Simon was born. Before the Rapture, and the 'home' is Earth, or after, and the 'home' is Eden. Regardless, I'm not sure why Simon was unenthusiastic about it. Maybe he just didn't believe her and couldn't share in her hope.

The SM-8 voices say "\[you dream of] going back to a mother who wouldn't recognise the killer she spawned. Or maybe she'd be proud". That's the main reason I think this is his mother at all, but more than this, she died *before* he turned into a monster. Which in itself seemed to happen at quite a young age. Maybe her death led him down that path.
### The COI (Consolidation)
I think the COI's knowledge about the blood moon is more limited than you'd expect. They're surprised when Simon finds a skeleton, even more so when he says something is alive down there. Then again, they knew this moon in particular was potentially important. So, what *did* they know about? The Light, I guess?

About Ava; David (Ava's second in command) says that Ava cares for everyone, albeit "some more than others". Whenever Simon accuses her of wanting him dead, she gets genuinely angry. I think if they could be remotely piloting these missions, they absolutely would. Despite what the Father says about them being secretive and exclusionary, Ava makes statements that imply Eden's people are included in her efforts to save humanity. And she does eventually apologise to Simon for being unable to save him. I can't really see her as evil.

### The Redhead (officially "The Guy Who Told You to Cross the Wires")

I think he's also the ghost-corpse in the sub, since he leads Simon to his own note. This role isn't just a 'voice' role, so he has to be on screen at *some* point, so at least one of these things must be true (lol)

At one point Simon asked Ava if anyone ever made it back up, and she simply said 'Yes' and didn't elaborate. At another point Ava mentions they've rescued subs before. I think this fellow chose to run out of oxygen ("I'll breathe my last") outside of the COI's influence ("uncontrolled") and they eventually recovered the sub, his body included. And now he's haunting it..?

### The Light
"It's what caused the Quiet Rapture, or a fragment of it". There was actually really interesting dialogue about it being the pinhole through which God, or *A* god views the universe, but I didn't manage to write it down.

After the data from the Light is downloaded to Simon's black box, the box itself starts unendingly gushing blood - I'm certain of this, there's a huge long shot on it to show us exactly that. So perhaps the 'answers' that the light provides aren't just revelations, they can be boiled down to hard data, and then that data supernaturally has an effect just by existing. Pretty cool. "It burst our mind like a tick" is the wording the SM-8 crew used referring to the answers from the Light, and ticks fill up with blood. Very clever word choice.

It would indeed be a great source of infinite air, water, and food (given that it's oxygenated blood, most likely). Nevermind that "the blood is human, it's US!"

I suppose that raises the question: does the Light still produce blood now that there's at least one, maybe two, copies of the data producing blood? Not an important question per se so we shan't dwell on it (pro dwelling on it state championship runner-up tshirt)

In addition to this, "We only saw \[signs of human blood] with the samples we took from near the light". So only a little bit of the blood ocean is human! Hooray. I guess that's handy to explain why they don't just use the blood already in the blood ocean. Honestly, the raging infections should have been clue number one.

### Themes, broadly
The will to survive at any cost, balancing the values of lives including your own, finding absolution or serving penance, staying true to your promises, justice, freedom. There's a lot going on.

A quick aside before I get into my main point: A few times 'free' is implied to equal 'dead'. The Brother choosing to die "uncontrolled". The Demon voices offer that "You will live and we will be free" and later *I think* they say "give us our peace". To which Simon says "Fine, you want the butcher!?" if that's his reply to their plea for mercy killing it's a WILD way to do it, but maybe him killing them is freeing them. That line wasn't in the CC though so it's anyone's guess. Ava also promises him that he'll be "free" and though I do believe she intended to rescue him it certainly doesn't end that way. Just generally the comparisons of the blood ocean to Hell give the idea that death would be better. Following this logic, the Demon's offer of eternal life also comes with implied imprisonment.

Another quick aside, there's no shortage of slightly Christian imagery throughout. I think Simon made a deal with the devil, or at least there's symbolism of the sort. The voice from the SM-8 asks him what he'd give to survive and he says "Everything". Later, the Demon simply says "Agreed". You could look at the bathing in blood as a kind of fucked up baptism. The Light as a God, the Rapture with most of humanity gone into said Light to be 'served within us', Eden is... Eden. Other people have pointed out Simon making that cross pose and being a sacrificial saviour figure. I don't know enough about christianity to tell you what it might *mean* but it's neat!

Simon clings to his will to live right up until the end of the movie:

"Sorry brother, but I wanna live"
"I'm not dying down here"
(multiple lines of bravado like "you're gonna have to come in here to kill me")

He has a strong sense for justice but doesn't apply it to himself much;

You see this in the microcosm of him irradiating the crew and David dropping the sub; "kinda fair after what you did". Simon accepts this wordlessly, and later apologises. He believes that reaction was fair. Yet, Simon yells at Ava that it's not 'fair' how he's being treated, despite having murdered plenty of people ("\[you've got] more blood on your hands than anyone out here, killer!" or whatever) even before Filament Station. His sense of justice is at odds with his desperation to live, because I mean, he's a *mass murderer*.

I can't pick if "I think I've done enough" is an honest feeling, or him just begging to be let out. Maybe he truly felt redeemed at that stage, but I kind of doubt it.

The Demon offers answers and, more importantly, eternal life. "You will live" plus the 'living proof' in the SM-8 crew.
It plays with his insecurities, "Half measures… never enough". (By the way, I really want that soundbyte, it went so hard). His indecision likely caused the Filament Station disaster and seems to be a sore point.
Ava comes down for him and the Demon kills her, blaming Simon. "You took her down to hell with you" ("and now she'll die" or something). The Demon dangles absolution in front of him. "You can still redeem yourself, destroy the data, none can be allowed to see the light again." The Demon tries to break him down and then offer an 'out', threatening him with death, then offering salvation. Threatening him with guilt, then offering forgiveness.

Despite all of this, Simon chooses self sacrifice, to believe "It's more than me". I wonder if he felt he'd done enough.
### in conclusion

In the end, Simon's eye flashes red, the ship explodes with red light and some dark tendrils shoot outwards and down… I'd buy those being roots, though they had red light going along them. The Demon fell to pieces, which is interesting.

The box *is* found at the end, Simon might have genuinely saved humanity, provided the 'answers' don't drive everyone insane.

Just by the by, there's another angle on the Light/Demon/SM-8 crew love triangle, where the SM-8 crew actually live within the Light, and the Demon is just some worm thing. That's pretty compelling considering lines like "The light thinks and therefore we are". It could be a reason why the SM-8 crew talk about the Demon as a seperate entity. I'm torn between the two ideas so you'll have to forgive me for sticking my own spokes this late in the game.

I can't wait to pick through this movie on streaming.
