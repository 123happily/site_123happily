I saw Iron Lung twice!
The second time, with subtitles in a different theatre. And to keep from being so terribly scared, I took notes. The *whole time*. I promise I wasn't being a nuisance to other people in the cinema.

Because of that, I think I put together a few things that wouldn't be obvious from a first watch, especially if you're like me and have difficulty hearing dialogue in movies.

This assumes you've already seen Iron Lung; both the game and the movie, and particularly the lore update to the game.

---
**Simon**
I think Simon's role as the 'butcher' was working for Eden to provide bodies to be used as soil to grow / maintain their trees. At the very least he killed a lot of people, perhaps at a young age, we see him surrounded by bodies with blood on his hands. These might be the other children we see in the flashback, I'd have to go frame-by-frame or something. He breaks from Eden over the plan to destroy Filament Station, yet is unable, or perhaps unwilling, to prevent it.

I think Simon made a deal with the devil for eternal life in the midpoint of the film, that's the symbolism I'm seeing anyway. The voice from the SM-8 asks him what he'd give to survive, and he says 'everything. I never had anything in the first place' looking at the glass charm. Later, the demon creature simply tells him "agreed".

That glass charm, I think it's got a petrified leaf or maybe even a germinating tree seed inside of it. He ties it onto his wrist, then in the dream-sequence-scene confronting the blood god his arm is tied down by that same string. It sinks into the blood, it's tethering him... I don't know what it means but it's cool!

Something about his eyes is important. The voices during the final act just says 'your eyes', and we see them flash red once at least, maybe twice. Including at the end right before the explosion/flash of light that rips apart the sub and the creature both.

**Eden + The Father**
From the game's lore update, it says Eden is a station with 468 people and the 'only known remaining trees' ("We wont be returning to the grove, brother")

Eden seems like a religious place - name aside. 'do you believe in god?' 'I hear enough of that talk on Eden'. It also seems to be a brothership - the Father, likely the leader of Eden, refers to the group we see Simon in as his 'sons'. They in turn refer to each other as 'brothers'. I personally think they're not all biological brothers, on ground of there being too many of them (lol).

The Father is the source of the opening monologue. ‘The consolidation hides its technology, its people’. He mentions that the COI knows that one of the blood moons is different to the rest, and I think it's implied that the COI is trying to hide that knowledge.

The kids were clearly indoctrinated from a very young age though, being told about the stars being gone and that their bodies will become the soil.

‘No-one on Eden wants to think this way, but \[what if it was the stations and ships that went missing, not the planets, and everyone else is just wondering where we went]’ I wonder why Eden doesnt want to think that... I guess their belief is that humanity is OVER over. Or at least that hope is dangerous/bad. Or maybe just that they shouldn't try to mess with the origin of the Quiet Rapture. Maybe they think of it as god's plan...?

'It's all over, that's what Eden understands' also doesn't tell me much more. I do wonder if Simon even agrees with this thought.

David says he read Simon's file, knows that he doesn't buy all of Eden's 'bullshit', "all that dog(talk?) about the last tree". So although what Simon says makes me think Eden are doomers, these lines seem to contradict that.

**Filament Station**
One of the COI's stations, maybe their biggest, but Eden is a much larger station. Eden decided to attack Filament Station for whatever reason. Per the game's lore update, "a 9 day battle on the station. The conflict ended unresolved when Eden troops resorted to sabotaging the station's reactor, resulting in a critical breach". This wording makes me think Eden didn't go in planning to destroy it.

We see a little of the event in Simon's flashbacks. A mentor giving him a radio, or a detonator. The redhead yelling at Simon to "Do something" before an explosion floods down the corridor and envelops him.

It's interesting to compare how Simon talks about Filament Station and how others talk about it.

Simon: "I didnt blow up Filament Station but the ones who did arent being punished" "\[I] didn't have a choice" "I had nothing to do with Filament Station, that wasn't part of the plan, they didnt listen to me" "People I thought were my brothers abandoned me"

Some lines I remember but didn't write down exactly, was that he "drew the short straw" trading one metal box to die in with another. Other lines like "Why does anyone care", "Filament Station is gone just like everything else in the Quiet Rapture"

Ava: "62 others didnt make it out and that doesnt include your compatriots"
Simon: "I didnt want them to die"

We've got all the hallmarks of a guilty conscience. Shifting the blame away, victimising himself, diminishing the damage.

Redhead in his memory, I think: "When did you grow a conscience, Simon? You’ve got the highest bodycount out here!" "\[You'll have to] kill your own brothers to stop this"

So I think Simon decided too late, or was too uncertain, to stop whatever happened on Filament Station. I would even wager he made it worse because of that.

Also.
I wish I remembered Simon's wording, whether it was 'I'll go to prison' or 'I'll go *back* to prison'. Because the official timeline of events puts Filament Station's destruction 5 years after the Quiet Rapture, and Simon is quite young during that time; so has it been like 20 years before the movie starts? Would the SM-8, who don't know about Filament Station, really have been down there for so long? This is based on numbers from the lore update of the game so I wouldn't be surprised or even really mad if they ignored those for the story.

**COI, broadly**
I think the COI's knowledge about the blood moon is more limited than expected. They're surprised when Simon finds a skeleton, even more so when he says something is alive down there. Then again, they knew this moon in particular was potentially important, so that surprise might have been faked.

I think if they could be remotely piloting these missions, they absolutely would.

The game lore says they're quite defensive and scared of Eden, meanwhile Eden is bitching that the COI doesn't share more information and resources, so the conflict is pretty understandable.

I think Ava learns some sympathy if not empathy throughout the movie, though she isn't starting from having *none*. David (her second in command) says that she cares for everyone, "some more than others". Whenever Simon accuses her of just wanting him dead, she gets genuinely angry. And she does eventually apologise to Simon for being unable to save him. I can't really see her as a villain.

**The Redhead (officially "The Guy Who Told You to Cross the Wires**
**")**
The guy who left the note, recording, and glass charm in the sub before Simon was put in. I think this is the redhead who shows up in Simon's flashback yelling at him to 'do something'. I think he's also the hallucinated corpse in the sub. This role isn't just a 'voice' role, so he has to be on screen at some point. I'm terrible at recognising faces so that is my best evidence :)

At one point Simon asked Ava if anyone ever made it back up, and she simply said 'Yes' and didn't elaborate. At another point Ava mentions they've rescued subs before. I think this fellow chose to run out of oxygen ("I'll breathe my last") somehow outside of the COI's influence ("uncontrolled") and they eventually recovered the sub, his body included. It's of course possible that they've had people return alive, perhaps with their minds broken, and/or recovered subs where people *didn't* "make it" back. All of these things could be true.

**The Mother (officially "A Familiar Voice")**
His mother seems like a cool character. I caught a line from who I think is her about the COI or some other group confiscating tree seeds, but that she'd snuck this one away. They (Her and Simon I think) would have their own garden. Then, "We have a tree, it'll feel just like home. Don't be like that, you’ll love it there" which means different things depending on if Simon was born before or after the Rapture: before, and the 'home' was Earth, or after, the 'home' was Eden, but his mother is setting up a new place somewhere else. The alien blood creature voice says "\[you want to] go back to a mother who wouldn't recognise the killer she spawned. Or maybe she'd be proud". That's the main reason I think this is his mother at all.

**Themes, broadly**
There's a lot of ideas in this movie. Will to survive at any cost, realising things are more important than your life, finding absolution/serving penance for your actions, staying true to your promises. Simon is holding desperately to his will to live right up until the end of the movie. *That's* what breaks in him.
‘Sorry brother, but I wanna live’
'I'm not dying down here'
‘Would you give everything just to survive?’ ‘Yeah. I never had anything to begin with’
He's offered eternal life by the Demon.
The Demon insults him and plays with his insecurities. "Half measures… never enough". Him being unable to stop what happened on Filament station because he couldn't fully turn against his brothers is probably a sore point for him considering how much he insists that it wasn't his fault/didn't mean for it to end that way.
Ava comes down after him and the Demon kills her, and then blames Simon. "You took her down to hell with you" Something in all of this breaks him. He chooses faith in Ava who lied to him over and over, he chooses self sacrifice; to believe 'It's more than me'. Maybe he feels that it's the only way to truly redeem himself.

He also says *multiple* times that he believes he's served his penance. "I think I've done enough". You see it in the microcosm of him irradiating the crew and David dropping the sub: "kinda fair after what you did". Crime, punishment, then it's 'fair'. Simon yells at Ava that it's not 'fair' what he's expected to do. But eventually he gives himself up for the greater good. I wonder if he felt absolved. The Demon tells him 
"You can still redeem yourself, destroy the data, none can be allowed to see the light again" I think the use of the word 'redeem' is very intentional.
